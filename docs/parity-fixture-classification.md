One row per group: the 1.2 M-rule it pins, what `docs/beermat-2.1-re.md` says about that rule in 2.1, and a verdict of **keep** (every pinned rule is unchanged in 2.1), **retire** (every pinned rule is replaced by a different 2.1 rule), **split** (it pins rules on both sides), or **needs human decision** (the 2.1 doc does not settle a rule it exercises, so the verdict cannot be derived without a follow-up).

The 1.2 rules come from `docs/beermat-re.md` "Mismatch index" (M-01 to M-13). The 2.1 verdicts come from `docs/beermat-2.1-re.md` "1.2 findings re-checked against 2.1" (with the N-xx ID where one exists). A group "pins" a rule when a step or a runner helper in its `.jsonl` exercises the behaviour that rule describes.

## How to read the verdicts

- **Keep** means the pinned behaviour is byte-for-byte the same in 2.1 and the group stays a parity gate unchanged.
- **Retire** means the pinned behaviour is a rule 2.1 replaced with a different one; the group's golden no longer describes 2.1. Retiring is not deleting — it records that the group must not be replayed against a 2.1 port as-is.
- **Split** means the group pins several behaviours and only some of them survived to 2.1. The kept steps form a valid 2.1 gate; the retired steps must be dropped (or moved to a 2.1-specific fixture) for the group to remain a gate.
- **Needs human decision** means the 2.1 doc does not settle a rule the group exercises, or does not settle the fate of an aggregate fixture — the verdict cannot be derived from `docs/beermat-2.1-re.md` alone.

- The wealth-cap mugging in 04 and the gun-dealer removal in 05/12 are 2.1 removals recorded in the "Arrival events" section of `docs/beermat-2.1-re.md`, not N-id rows; they are why those groups retire.
- 08 and 09 are the only two "needs human decision" groups, and both are about state-layout / aggregate-gate scope rather than an individual rule the 2.1 doc left unresolved.
